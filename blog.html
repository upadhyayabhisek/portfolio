e-Governance Challenges in Nepal Case Study

June 19, 2025

TLDR: Nepal’s e-Governance initiatives, like the Nagarik App, National Identity Card, and e-Passport system, have faced challenges related to integration, digital literacy, security, and physical distribution, despite improving government efficiency and transparency.

Read More

1. Introduction
1.1 Overview of E-Governance
E-Governance refers to the process of using the internet and digital technologies to provide services offered by the government. This allows us to improve transparency within the government and the efficiency of services provided by the government. It encourages citizen engagement while also helping to reduce corruption within the government departments. It also allows for services such as tax filing, passport and license applications, as well as renewals, to be made through the internet. It improves data transfer between government departments.

1.2 Case Studies Introduction
Nepal has implemented multiple E-Governance initiatives since the early 2000s. The E-Governance initiatives which have been chosen for this study include Nagarik App, National Identity Card, and ePassport System, which were launched through the Digital Nepal Framework act in 2019. By examining the impact and outcomes of these initiatives, as well as the challenges faced by them, we can gain insights into the process of E-Governance as well as learn about the Digitalization of Nepal.

2. Case Studies Initiative Implemented in Nepal
2.1 Nagarik App
The Nagarik App is a mobile application developed by the Nepal Government. This app was created to provide a simpler and easier way to deliver government services to citizens through the internet. Multiple services are offered through the app, including complaints, bill and tax payments, and documentation management.

2.1.1 Impact
Nagarik App has enhanced government services through digitizing and centralizing services. It has also helped remove delays and improve efficiency. For services such as PAN card registration, bill payments, etc., which used to take multiple hours and long queues, can now be done within a few hours. Nagarik App has also helped increase public trust and awareness about government services.

2.1.2 Outcome

  • It helps reduce corruption due to increased transparency.
  • Provides access to government services for people in rural areas.
  • Allows for reduced delays in public services.

2.1.3 Challenges Faced
  • Government workers and officials have been slow to embrace this change.
  • Services weren’t fully integrated at the launch of the app, resulting in concerns about security.
  • Older citizens with limited technological knowledge were not able to utilize it.

2.2 National Identity Card Project
The National Identity Card Project was started by DoNIDCR to create a biometric-based identification system for all Nepali citizens. It was started to improve digital governance and security.

2.2.1 Impact
The National Identity Card Project has allowed Nepal to push forward towards digitization. By having a biometric identity card, it replaces traditional documents such as the citizenship card. This allows the same document to be used for all services that require government identification, such as banking, social security, SIM card registration, passport registration, etc. This process also ensures that the data provided for services is accurate.

2.2.2 Outcome
  • It allows for providing services securely and efficiently.
  • It prevents fake documents from being used to gain access to certain services.
  • Ensures the same data is used for all services, such as banking, land registration, etc.

2.2.3 Challenges Faced
  • Physical distribution of the card is still lacking.
  • Concerns over the security of collected data.
  • People with limited computer knowledge find it hard to fill out digital forms.
  • Due to its mandatory status, citizens find it hard to get social services.

2.3 e-Passport System
The e-Passport System was started in 2021 by the Department of Passports to help modernize and digitize the country’s passports. The e-Passport System uses biometric data and an electronic chip to ensure security and meet international passport standards.

2.3.1 Impact
The e-Passport System has allowed Nepal to achieve a secure travel document that meets international standards. As e-Passports have biometric chips, it reduces the chances of people using fake passports or committing identity fraud. This also allows for faster immigration processing and more efficient border control. Since the National ID is required for the e-Passport, the same data is used in multiple departments.

2.3.2 Outcome
  • Modernization of the country’s passport.
  • Improved security with biometric documents.
  • Increased efficiency in processing and printing passports.

2.3.3 Challenges Faced
  • Concerns over data security.
  • Lack of transparency regarding procurement.
  • NPR 2 billion was lost due to wrong passport distribution.

3. Comparative Analysis
3.1 Comparative Analysis with Bangladesh (Digital Bangladesh)
3.1.1 What Nepal Did Better
  • Centralized mobile app with over 50 services.
  • Data privacy by avoiding third-party developers.

3.1.2 What Bangladesh Did Better
  • Developed a more large-scale services system.
  • Over 150 services offered, including health, education, land, etc.

3.2 Comparative Analysis with Sri Lanka (e-Passport)
3.2.1 What Nepal Did Better
  • Used a centralized application and tracking system.
  • Faster processing and passport delivery time.

3.2.2 What Sri Lanka Did Better
  • Transparency with the procurement process.

3.3 Comparative Analysis with Cambodia (National ID)
3.3.1 What Nepal Did Better
  • Made the National ID mandatory for all services offered.
  • Digital ID rollout before physical ID through the Nagarik App.

3.3.2 What Cambodia Did Better
  • Faster nationwide physical card rollout.
  • Multi-language support for the registration form.

4. Recommendations for Improvement
  • Awareness and training programs should be provided to people with limited technical knowledge.
  • The application process should support multiple commonly used languages in Nepal.
  • There should be transparency during the procurement process.
  • Public data security should be prioritized.
  • Allow local companies to work with foreign investors and companies to provide services locally.
  • Remote areas should be provided with proper internet infrastructure.
  • Officials should receive proper training and guidance to work with these systems.
