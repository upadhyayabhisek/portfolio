e-Governance Challenges in Nepal

A case study exploring Nepal's digital transformation, government technology initiatives and the challenges of building an interconnected digital nation.

THE SHORT VERSION

Nepal's e-Governance initiatives, including Nagarik App, National Identity Card and e-Passport, have improved access to government services. However, integration, digital literacy, data security and physical distribution remain significant challenges.

READ FULL ARTICLE ↗

Digital transformation is changing how governments deliver services. But implementing technology is only one part of the challenge.

01. Introduction

1.1 Overview of E-Governance

E-Governance refers to the use of internet-based technologies and digital systems to provide government services. It aims to improve transparency, efficiency and citizen engagement while reducing administrative delays and opportunities for corruption.

Services such as tax filing, passport applications, license renewals and payments can be delivered digitally. It also improves information exchange between government departments.

1.2 Case Study Introduction

Nepal has implemented multiple e-Governance initiatives since the early 2000s. This study focuses on three initiatives: Nagarik App, the National Identity Card and the e-Passport System.

02. E-Governance Initiatives in Nepal

2.1 Nagarik App

Nagarik App is a mobile application developed by the Government of Nepal to provide citizens with easier access to public services through the internet. Its services include complaints, bill and tax payments, and digital document management.

2.1.1 Impact

The application has helped centralize government services and reduce administrative delays. Services such as PAN registration and bill payments can be accessed without visiting multiple government offices.

2.1.2 Outcomes

  • Improved transparency in public services.
  • Reduced delays in government processes.
  • Improved access to services for citizens.

2.1.3 Challenges

  • Slow adoption among government officials.
  • Incomplete service integration during early releases.
  • Limited digital literacy among older citizens.

2.2 National Identity Card Project

The National Identity Card Project was initiated by the Department of National ID and Civil Registration to establish a biometric identification system for Nepali citizens.

2.2.1 Impact

The biometric identity system supports digital identification across government and private services. It can help improve data consistency for banking, social security, passport registration and other identification-based services.

2.2.2 Outcomes

  • More consistent citizen identification.
  • Improved verification and service security.
  • Reduced reliance on separate identification records.

2.2.3 Challenges

  • Delays in physical card distribution.
  • Concerns regarding personal data security.
  • Difficulty completing digital forms for some citizens.
  • Access barriers when services depend on National ID availability.

2.3 e-Passport System

Nepal introduced its e-Passport system in 2021. It uses biometric information and an electronic chip to improve document security and meet international passport standards.

2.3.1 Impact

Electronic passports improve document verification and help reduce the risk of identity fraud. They also support more efficient immigration processing and modern border management.

2.3.2 Outcomes

  • Modernization of Nepal's travel documents.
  • Improved document security through biometrics.
  • More standardized passport processing.

2.3.3 Challenges

  • Concerns surrounding personal data security.
  • Procurement transparency concerns.
  • Distribution and operational challenges.

03. Comparative Analysis

3.1 Nepal and Bangladesh — Digital Bangladesh

Where Nepal performed better:

  • A centralized mobile application for government services.
  • Greater emphasis on centralized service delivery.

Where Bangladesh performed better:

  • A broader ecosystem of digital public services.
  • More extensive service coverage across sectors.

3.2 Nepal and Sri Lanka — e-Passport

Where Nepal performed better:

  • A centralized application and tracking process.
  • Improvements in application management.

Where Sri Lanka performed better:

  • Greater emphasis on procurement transparency.

3.3 Nepal and Cambodia — National ID

Where Nepal performed better:

  • Integration of digital identity into public services.
  • Digital identity access through the Nagarik App.

Where Cambodia performed better:

  • Faster nationwide physical card distribution.
  • Multilingual registration support.

04. Recommendations for Improvement

Nepal's digital governance initiatives have considerable potential. To make them more effective and inclusive, several improvements are necessary:

  • Provide digital awareness and training programs.
  • Support commonly used languages in Nepal.
  • Improve transparency in government procurement.
  • Prioritize public data security and privacy.
  • Encourage collaboration between local and international technology companies.
  • Improve internet infrastructure in remote regions.
  • Provide government officials with technical training.

05. Final Thoughts

Digital governance is not simply about moving government services online. It is about making those services accessible, secure, reliable and useful to everyone.

Nepal has made meaningful progress, but successful digital transformation requires better integration, stronger infrastructure, public trust and continued investment in digital literacy.
